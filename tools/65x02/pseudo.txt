# Pseudo-instruction registry: what each rule in src/isa/pseudo.asm costs today
# and what its proposed hardware encoding would cost. This is what lets a slice
# be scored BEFORE it is built - `migrate_ext.py --pseudo` multiplies these by
# the site counts it finds.
#
# `now` is measured, not assumed: the expansion's cycles come from
# docs/cpu-timing-v2.json, which is THIS core's timing, and this core's
# branches are a flat 2 cycles with no taken or page-cross penalty. The
# add-isa-test-and-branch proposal costed the same sequence at 8 cycles using
# NMOS numbers; it is 7 here. Scoring a slice against the wrong baseline
# inflates it, which is exactly what gate G4 exists to stop.
#
# `hw` is the PROPOSAL's encoding, and stays a claim until silicon exists.
#
# pseudo   now_b now_c   hw_b hw_c_min hw_c_max   slice
cbeq         6     7       4      5        6      add-isa-test-and-branch
cbne         6     7       4      5        6      add-isa-test-and-branch
cblt         6     7       4      5        6      add-isa-test-and-branch
cbge         6     7       4      5        6      add-isa-test-and-branch
tbz          6     7       4      5        6      add-isa-test-and-branch
tbnz         6     7       4      5        6      add-isa-test-and-branch

# The zero tests. `lda v / beq t` is already 4 bytes and 5 cycles, and the
# proposed CBEQ zp,#0,rel is 4 bytes and 5-6 cycles - so this idiom saves no
# space and may cost a cycle. It buys the accumulator and nothing else. Listed
# so the slice is scored on what it buys rather than on how often it appears.
bzero        4     5       4      5        6      add-isa-test-and-branch
bnzero       4     5       4      5        6      add-isa-test-and-branch

# add-isa-word-ops, already built. Kept as the calibration entry: the projection
# below should reproduce the migration's actual result of 6 bytes per site.
addw16      12    18       6     12       12      add-isa-word-ops
